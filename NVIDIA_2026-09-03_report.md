# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) — Expanded Valuation Research Report

**Research date:** September 3, 2026  
**Intended user:** Student equity analyst deciding whether to initiate, watch-defer, or decline an investment in NVIDIA.  
**Valuation date:** September 3, 2026  
**Currency and units:** U.S. dollars; financial-statement amounts are in millions unless stated otherwise.

## Decision frame

NVIDIA is a U.S.-listed semiconductor and accelerated-computing platform company whose common stock trades on Nasdaq under NVDA. Its principal platforms address Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, and Automotive markets. ([FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000023/nvda-20250126.htm))

The screening policy requires positive EBIT in each of the last five fiscal years, positive FCFF in at least four of five years, and adequate disclosure for a defensible enterprise-value forecast. It also disqualifies a company if one customer represents more than 30% of revenue. The expanded research supports continued study of NVIDIA, but it does not yet establish a purchase price because a complete DCF, peer-multiple range, and per-share bridge have not been completed.

## What changed from Edition A

The frozen Edition A described FY2026 revenue only as being in a “$130B+ range.” The audited FY2026 figure is **$215.938 billion**; **$130.497 billion** was FY2025 revenue. This correction comes directly from NVIDIA’s FY2026 Form 10-K and is maintained here rather than changing the submitted baseline. ([FY2026 Form 10-K, consolidated statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm))

The expanded source set also resolves the literal customer-disqualifier test more cleanly. NVIDIA disclosed that its largest direct customer represented 22% of FY2026 revenue and another represented 14%; the largest disclosed direct-customer percentage was therefore below the policy’s 30% single-customer threshold. Concentration nevertheless increased from FY2024, when one customer represented 13%, and FY2025, when one represented 12% and two others represented 11% each. ([FY2026 Form 10-K, “Concentration of Revenue”](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm); [FY2024 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000029/nvda-20240128.htm))

## Multi-year operating evidence

NVIDIA’s audited revenue rose from $26.974 billion in FY2023 to $60.922 billion in FY2024, $130.497 billion in FY2025, and $215.938 billion in FY2026. Audited operating income was positive throughout the comparable history: $4.224 billion in FY2023, $32.972 billion in FY2024, $81.453 billion in FY2025, and $130.387 billion in FY2026. These figures strongly support the durable-profitability screen, although the complete five-year table should still be reconstructed under one consistent convention before final scoring. ([FY2023 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581023000017/nvda-20230129.htm); [FY2024 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581024000029/nvda-20240128.htm); [FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000023/nvda-20250126.htm); [FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm))

Cash generation also expanded sharply. Net cash from operations was $28.090 billion in FY2024, $64.089 billion in FY2025, and $102.718 billion in FY2026. Purchases of property, equipment, and intangible assets were $1.069 billion, $3.236 billion, and $6.042 billion in those years, respectively. A simple operating-cash-flow-minus-capital-purchases measure was therefore positive in all three periods, but this is not a substitute for the assignment’s explicit FCFF calculation and should not be mislabeled as FCFF. ([FY2026 Form 10-K, cash-flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm))

At January 25, 2026, NVIDIA reported $10.605 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $51.951 billion of marketable securities, or $62.556 billion combined. Its notes had a net carrying amount of $8.468 billion, including a $0.999 billion short-term portion. This indicates a substantial net liquid-asset position before considering other claims and investments, but a full enterprise-to-equity bridge must define which securities and non-operating assets are included rather than automatically treating every marketable security as excess cash. ([FY2026 Form 10-K, liquidity and debt notes](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm))

FY2026 diluted weighted-average shares were 24.514 billion, compared with 24.804 billion in FY2025 and 24.940 billion in FY2024. A per-share valuation should use the appropriate diluted convention and valuation-date share basis rather than mixing weighted-average diluted shares with period-end basic shares. ([FY2026 Form 10-K, EPS note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm))

## Current operating momentum

The latest reported quarter strengthens the operating thesis. For Q2 FY2027, ended July 26, 2026, NVIDIA reported $96.221 billion of revenue, up 106% year over year, and $63.734 billion of GAAP operating income, up 124%. Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion, or roughly 92% of consolidated quarterly revenue, showing both exceptional AI-infrastructure demand and significant dependence on that market. NVIDIA guided to Q3 FY2027 revenue of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, with no Data Center compute revenue from China included in the outlook. ([NVIDIA Q2 FY2027 results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx))

Management said Vera Rubin was in full production and emphasized growing demand across hyperscalers, AI labs, sovereign customers, neoclouds, enterprises, and edge deployments. On the August 26 earnings call, management also acknowledged that frontier AI labs are growing faster than their balance sheets and credit profiles can support and that NVIDIA shipped less than 1% of Data Center revenue in H200 products to China during Q2; China Data Center compute revenue was excluded from the forward outlook. These statements support a large demand opportunity while also highlighting customer-financing and geopolitical risks. ([Q2 FY2027 earnings-call transcript](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/content_files/TRANSCRIPT_-NVIDIA-Corp-NVDA-US-Q2-2027-Earnings-Call-26-August-2026-5_00-PM-ET.pdf))

Recent independent reporting provides a useful counterweight to management’s outlook. Reuters reported that NVIDIA forecast 70% revenue growth for the fiscal year ending January 2028, while warning that memory-component shortages would constrain expansion. Reuters also reported investor concern about NVIDIA’s financial support for parts of the AI ecosystem and whether such arrangements make demand less independent and sustainable. ([Reuters, August 26, 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-above-estimates-4877887); [Reuters, August 25, 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-faces-growth-test-as-rubin-debut-meets-ai-financing-scrutiny-4874842))

## Thesis, risks, and unresolved valuation work

The favorable case rests on audited revenue and operating-income expansion, very strong operating cash generation, a net liquid-asset position, and continuing demand for the Blackwell and Vera Rubin platforms. The company also remains profitable despite the FY2023 gaming and inventory correction, supporting the screen’s durable-profitability requirement. ([FY2023 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581023000017/nvda-20230129.htm); [FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm))

The main risks are customer concentration, dependence on Data Center spending, export controls and uncertain China access, supply and memory constraints, rapid product-transition execution, and the credit quality of AI customers that require outside financing. The 22% largest-customer disclosure does not trigger the literal 30% disqualifier, but the direction of concentration is unfavorable and should remain a monitored thesis variable. ([FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm); [Q2 FY2027 earnings-call transcript](https://investor.nvidia.com/files/content_files/TRANSCRIPT_-NVIDIA-Corp-NVDA-US-Q2-2027-Earnings-Call-26-August-2026-5_00-PM-ET.pdf))

The decisive missing work is valuation rather than company quality. A defensible investment decision still requires normalized FCFF, an explicit forecast, a discount rate, terminal assumptions, sensitivity analysis, a consistent enterprise-to-equity bridge, valuation-date diluted shares, and comparison with the market price. Until those calculations establish a margin of safety, strong operating performance alone is not enough to label the stock a buy.

## Current call

**WATCH-DEFER (wait for more information).** NVIDIA passes the literal customer-concentration screen and shows extraordinary audited growth, profitability, and cash generation, but I would not initiate a purchase until a reproducible DCF/multiples range and per-share bridge demonstrate a meaningful margin of safety under conservative growth, margin, and customer-financing assumptions.

**What would change the call:** I would change to **INITIATE** if the completed valuation shows material upside under conservative assumptions without a customer exceeding the 30% threshold; I would change to **DO NOT INITIATE** if concentration, financing exposure, or normalized cash-flow evidence invalidates those assumptions.
