### Why the results differ

The final outputs can be placed beside one another because they value the same company's common equity per share in USD on the same date. Their economic bases differ, however. Market prices may reflect expectations for growth beyond the completed annual reporting periods; dividing those prices by older earnings can produce high multiples. Different business mixes and reported earnings items can also affect the comparison. These are plausible explanations, not a quantified reconciliation proving which factor causes the gap. The filings and current calculations do not establish how much of the difference comes from growth expectations, earnings timing, accounting or relative mispricing. The annual reported EPS basis remains unchanged for this lab.

I **withhold an unconditional investment fair-value range** and do not adopt the $378.60–$935.14 peer output as one. The $225.73 market price lies inside the conditional DCF envelope, so the base-case downside alone does not settle the investment decision. No average of the DCF and peer methods is used. Sources D–F above provide the underlying results and assumptions.

### Answer to the skeptical question

The question was: “What evidence in the sources would justify applying even Broadcom's lower 77.27× annual reported P/E to NVIDIA, rather than treating it as a consequence of Broadcom's different earnings mix and reporting period?”

**The sources checked so far do not justify that transfer.** Broadcom's filing establishes AI-compute/networking overlap, but also documents infrastructure software as 42% of FY2025 revenue. NVIDIA's CUDA differentiation could matter to relative value, but a product advantage is not evidence for a particular numerical multiple. I therefore retain Broadcom as a qualified comparison, not as proof that NVIDIA is worth at least $378.60. Nor do I claim that software mix or timing alone explains Broadcom's multiple; that causal explanation remains unresolved.

### Evidence that would change the decision

- **Support for initiating:** a documented cost-of-capital estimate and an operating forecast grounded in disclosed revenue, margins, taxes and reinvestment that still produce a sufficient margin of safety under conservative scenarios at the purchase price. A lower market price could also change the decision after rechecking the fundamentals. No numerical entry threshold is established by the present evidence.
- **Support for assigning more weight to the peer result:** a source-supported comparison of the peers' and NVIDIA's growth expectations, recurring earnings composition, risk and fiscal timing that explains why a transferable annual reported P/E is reasonable. This would strengthen interpretation; it would not silently replace the lab's reported EPS with adjusted or quarterly earnings.
- **Support for do not initiate:** source-backed evidence of persistently weaker growth, lower sustainable margins or greater reinvestment requirements that leaves the market price above a defensible valuation even after resolving discount-rate uncertainty.

Until that evidence is available, WATCH-DEFER reflects insufficient support for an entry valuation, rather than automatic acceptance of either method's point estimate.
