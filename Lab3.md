\*\*User:\*\* Me, acting as a student equity analyst selecting one company for Project 1\.

\*\*Decision:\*\* Whether NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) is the correct selection from the defined universe, screened per the criteria below, to serve as the subject of a defensible enterprise-value forecast.

\*\*Screening methodology applied (as specified):\*\*  
\- \*\*Universe:\*\* U.S.-listed, non-financial operating companies with ≥5 years of public financials.  
\- \*\*Exclusions:\*\* Banks, insurers, REITs, funds, shells, pre-revenue firms.  
\- \*\*Criterion 1 — Durable profitability:\*\* Positive EBIT in each of the last 5 fiscal years.  
\- \*\*Criterion 2 — Cash conversion:\*\* Positive FCFF in ≥4 of those 5 years.  
\- \*\*Ranking:\*\* 60% weight on 5-year average ROIC, 40% weight on 5-year FCFF stability, among companies clearing both minimums.  
\- \*\*Success test:\*\* Sufficient operating history/disclosure to forecast EV without relying primarily on speculative growth.  
\- \*\*Overriding disqualifier:\*\* Exclude if latest filings show one customer \>30% of revenue.

\#\# 3\. Initial Thesis

\*\*INITIATE — provisionally.\*\*

NVDA appears to clear both quantitative minimums by a wide margin (EBIT positive across all 5 years; FCFF positive in essentially all 5), ranks at or near the top of any reasonable peer set on 5-year average ROIC, and has five-plus years of audited, detailed segment disclosure — satisfying the "sufficient operating history" success test. The overriding disqualifier does \*\*not\*\* appear to trigger on the letter of the rule (no single customer exceeded 30% of revenue in the most recent 10-K), but customer concentration is rising sharply and is close enough to the threshold that it needs explicit verification and monitoring before treating this as a clean pass — see Section 5\.

\#\# 5\. Consequential Assumptions

1\. \*\*"One customer \>30%" is read literally (single named/designated customer, not aggregated top-N).\*\* Under this reading, NVDA passes — the highest disclosed single-customer figure is 22% (FY2026). If the disqualifier were intended to capture \*aggregate\* concentration among a small number of customers, NVDA would very plausibly fail (top 4 \= 61% in the most recent quarter) — this is a genuine judgment call the screen's wording leaves open, and it's the single most consequential assumption in this selection.  
2\. \*\*The FY2023 ROIC dip (down to \~11% under one methodology) does not represent a break in "durable profitability"\*\* — it coincided with a post-crypto/gaming-inventory correction rather than a structural EBIT decline into negative territory. EBIT itself is assumed to have stayed positive that year (not directly confirmed above — flagged for verification).  
3\. \*\*Reported ROIC figures across sources are directionally consistent despite methodology differences\*\* (NOPAT/invested-capital definitions vary by provider) — the qualitative ranking (very high, rising sharply post-FY2023) is assumed robust to those differences.  
4\. \*\*Non-GAAP vs. GAAP operating income distinctions are treated as immaterial to the pass/fail screen\*\* — both were positive in the years checked, but the ranking calculation should specify which basis is used.

\#\# 6\. Unknowns

\- \*\*Exact GAAP EBIT for each of the last 5 fiscal years (FY2022–FY2026), confirmed positive in all five\*\* — I have this for FY2021 and partial years; need FY2022–FY2026 pulled directly from 10-Ks.  
\- \*\*Exact FCFF (not FCFE or simple FCF) for each of the 5 years\*\* — the sources above give operating cash flow / capex-based FCF, which is close to but not identical to FCFF (which requires an unlevered adjustment for after-tax interest). Needs to be rebuilt precisely.  
\- \*\*Whether the \>30% disqualifier is meant to apply to a single named customer or to concentrated aggregate exposure\*\* — this is a rule-interpretation question, not a data question, and materially changes the initiate/exclude call.  
\- \*\*Full peer set that also cleared both minimums\*\*, to confirm NVDA actually ranks at the top on the 60/40 ROIC/FCFF-stability weighting rather than assuming it by reputation.  
\- \*\*FCFF stability metric definition\*\* — the criteria specify "40% on five-year FCFF stability" without defining stability (e.g., std. deviation, coefficient of variation, or count of positive years) — needs to be pinned down before scoring.  
\- \*\*Whether Automotive/Professional Visualization/Gaming segments have their own customer concentration risk\*\*, distinct from the Data Center-driven 22%/14% figures, that could matter for segment-level EV forecasting even if the consolidated screen passes.
