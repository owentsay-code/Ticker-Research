# NVIDIA five year pro forma and valuation

**NVIDIA Corporation | NASDAQ NVDA | Prepared September 24, 2026**

This report adapts the ABG exercise to NVIDIA. It includes a three-year history grid, ratios, assumptions, four linked projected statements and a cash flow valuation. The base case produces **$209.28 per share**, with all five years showing positive FCFE. This is a scenario based on stated assumptions, not company guidance or a market price.

## Scope and dates

History uses FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026, each from its own 10-K. The projection uses the latest July 26, 2026 balance sheet and trailing twelve month revenue of $302.970 billion (215.938 + 177.837 − 90.805). Year 1–5 are full future annual periods from the September 24 modeling date, approximately through September 2031; they are not NVIDIA fiscal years. The July balance sheet is a proxy for the valuation-date balance sheet; the reporting lag is not separately rolled forward. This follows the rolling-period convention of the earlier NVIDIA report. [FY2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm) and [Q27](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx).

## Assumptions

The reasons below are draft analytical explanations for you to review and put into your own words. “History” labels observed facts; carrying a historical figure forward is labeled “Judgment.”

| Value | Label | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting revenue $302.970bn and July opening balances | history | Reported inputs from K26 and Q27; the TTM revenue bridge is shown above. |
| Quarterly outlook revenue $108bn ±2%; gross margin 74% ±0.5%; FY27 tax 16–18% | guidance | Q27 outlook provides a near-term reference, not a five-year forecast. |
| Revenue growth 45%, 30%, 20%, 15%, 10% | judgment | Retain the earlier NVIDIA scenario. Growth slows as the revenue base expands; Year 1 revenue of $439.3bn is near four times the $108bn quarterly outlook, but that annualization is only a reasonableness check. |
| Gross margin 74%, 73%, 72%, 71%, 70% | judgment | Begin near the quarterly outlook, then allow pricing and competition to reduce margins. |
| SG&A 3% of gross profit | judgment | Round the FY2026 ratio of about 2.98%; assume selling and administrative costs scale with gross profit. |
| EBIT margin 64%, 62%, 60%, 59%, 58%; R&D is the remaining operating expense | judgment | Retain the prior operating-margin path while allowing R&D intensity to rise. R&D equals gross profit less SG&A less EBIT; this is a scenario allocation, not independent company guidance. |
| D&A 1.2% of revenue; up to 15% of D&A assigned to existing intangibles | judgment | Use the earlier model’s rounded reinvestment assumption. Allocate the remainder to depreciation and cap amortization at the remaining intangible balance; this avoids depreciating intangible assets through PP&E. |
| Capital spending 3% of revenue, all new spending assigned to PP&E | judgment | Allow more spending than the recent annual purchases ratio to support growth. All-cash PP&E additions simplify the schedule; future acquired technology would require a separate intangible forecast. |
| Incremental operating working capital 20% of additional revenue | judgment | Retain the prior model’s allowance for receivables, inventory and supplier funding. Spread the net investment across accounts in proportion to their opening balances; this is not a separate inventory-days forecast. |
| Forecast tax rate 17% | judgment | Use the midpoint of the 16–18% outlook as a normalized rate and extend it beyond the guidance period. Cash tax equals book tax; no tax benefit is recognized on a loss. |
| Floor plan financing none; accounts payable modeled separately | history | No dealer floor plan balance is reported. Accounts payable represents supplier credit and must not be renamed floor plan debt. |
| Debt $33.366bn constant; net borrowing zero; interest 4% | judgment | Assume maturities are refinanced at equal principal. A normalized 4% cost avoids using the old interest expense on a materially larger current debt balance; no net new borrowing or refinancing fees are forecast. |
| Stock compensation 2.2% of revenue; offsetting buybacks equal that expense | judgment | Approximate the first-half stock compensation ratio. Buybacks offset the modeled expense economically; equal dollars do not establish the actual share dilution offset. |
| Dividends $24.285bn per year; share proxy 24.285bn fixed | judgment | Annualize the $0.25 quarterly dividend using the latest diluted-share proxy. Assume no net share change and no additional discretionary buybacks; this is not management’s capital-return plan. |
| Other assets and liabilities and securities fixed; investment income and gains zero; OCI zero | judgment | Isolate operating performance without forecasting investment returns or acquisitions. Excess cash accumulates with no assumed yield, understating income if it earns interest. |
| Minimum cash zero; no revolver | judgment | Require cash to remain nonnegative. Forecast operations fund spending and dividends; a cash deficit would require revising financing, not hiding it in equity. |
| WACC 10%; terminal growth 3.5% | judgment | Retain the earlier required return and long-run nominal growth assumptions. They are scenario inputs, not an independently estimated current CAPM result. |
| Terminal ratios at Year 5; working capital recalculated at 3.5% growth | judgment | Model a stable business with continued reinvestment; do not compound the unusually high-growth Year 5 working-capital charge into perpetuity. |
| Equity bridge +$66.003bn | judgment | Add opening cash and marketable securities, subtract debt; treat those assets as available at carrying value without realization tax. Exclude nonmarketable holdings from value pending a realizable-value assessment. |

Dividends and discretionary buybacks are uses of FCFE, not costs deducted to calculate standard FCFE.

## Losses and negative cash flow

NVIDIA has positive historical net income and positive historical operating cash flow less the combined capital-spending measure in all three years. All five projected economic FCFE values are positive, so the assignment’s negative-FCFE exception is not triggered.

If a revised scenario has negative FCFE, retain that year’s full statements and label it “negative FCFE.” If the assignment requires valuing only positive amounts, show the PV of positive flows as a separately labeled assignment subtotal; excluding funding deficits is not a complete equity valuation. Do not substitute FCFF for a negative FCFE without explanation.

**A perpetually negative terminal cash flow cannot support a meaningful positive going-concern equity value without a credible path to sustainable positive cash flow; the formula can return a negative number, but that is not a defensible positive terminal valuation.**
