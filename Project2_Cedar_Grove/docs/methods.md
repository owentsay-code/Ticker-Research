# Methods and limits

Decision information set: October 22, 2026. Report prepared November 17, 2026. These are distinct dates. Price months run October 2021–September 2026; monthly labels denote the represented month, not an executable first-day price. There are 60 prices and 59 returns. No live data download is needed to reproduce.

Monthly return = adjusted price / prior adjusted price − 1. Estimate annual arithmetic return as 12 times the monthly mean and annual covariance as 12 times sample covariance (n−1 denominator). Portfolio expected net return = weights dot annual estimates − annual incremental cost / 10,000. Volatility = square root of weights transpose × covariance × weights. This is neither a compound growth forecast nor a downside guarantee. Fund expenses embedded in adjusted returns are not deducted twice; 25 bp is an additional illustrative annual implementation/advisory drag, not an observed quote or a turnover-based trading-cost model.

The research target is 5.50%: assumed CPI of 2.50% plus the mandate's 3%. CPI is an assumption, not a claimed contemporary release. The mandate states a rolling five-year net target, which the arithmetic estimate cannot certify. The 59-return sample is too short for even one full 60-month window. There is no payout-path, rolling-five-year or out-of-sample performance claim.

The solver enumerates all 1,771 fully invested, long-only four-asset weights in 5-percentage-point increments. It filters the fund cap (all four holdings are funds, including SGOV), reserve interpretation, research return floor and signal equity cap, then selects the minimum annual variance. Ties retain the first enumerated point. The 3% reserve requirement rounds up to at least 5% on this grid. A feasible-grid minimum is not a continuous optimum, and grid infeasibility alone does not prove continuous infeasibility. Strict reserve infeasibility is stronger: none of this four-fund universe is cash or direct Treasury bills.

Equal weight is 25% in each fund. The second transparent baseline is 60% ACWI and 40% VCIT. That 60/40 mix is a **proposed evaluation benchmark**, adapted from the earlier Meridian exercise: the saved Cedar Grove mandate did not specify one. It is not represented as approved and is allowed to fail the investment constraints as a comparator. Estimated volatility is the research risk measure, minimized without an invented risk ceiling. The saved mandate explicitly lacks a numeric loss ceiling and leaves the risk objective unresolved. These are adoption blockers, not silent additions to Lab 13.

All four ETFs are assumed fully saleable with proceeds within five business days in normal markets. No live liquidity verification is available. The reserve proxy is used only for a conditional research portfolio. SGOV holds Treasury exposure but its shares are not direct bills: the strict reserve is zero. Full mandate verdict is therefore FAIL for every displayed portfolio, even when its modeled numerical rules pass.

The process stops on invalid data, no feasible grid portfolio, or any unresolved live-use governance requirement. It never relaxes caps to manufacture an answer. Quarterly monitoring must cover the fixed $60,000 reserve (recompute percentage as portfolio value changes), 95% liquidity and 40% fund caps; annual turnover uses the mandate's lesser-of-purchases-and-sales definition and actual average month-end values. Current holdings, transaction history and execution evidence are absent, so turnover, payout funding and rebalancing compliance are unverified. No recommended trades are sent.

Before live reconsideration, obtain written reserve eligibility/implementation, benchmark and risk approval; implement holdings, payouts and trade-history monitoring; approve assumptions and stress thresholds; then rerun under the approved rules. Recommendation is 0%–0% now, not a suggestion to liquidate existing holdings.
